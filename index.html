1. Probability of Detection (Pd)

Ratio of active emitter pulses successfully intercepted by the receiver dwell window divided by total pulse emissions across all channels.

Pd = Intercepted Pulses / Total Active Transmissions

2. Probability of False Alarm (Pfa)

Rate of false alarm triggers occurring when receiver dwells on quiet channels due to receiver noise floor spikes.

Pfa = False Detections / Total Quiet Dwells

3. Average Intercept Rate (AIR)

Normalized interception throughput metric reflecting total intercepted signals relative to continuous mission dwell clock intervals.

AIR = Intercepted Dwells / Total Mission Steps

4. Average Intercept Time Error (AITE)

Average temporal delay between the start of an emitter burst and the instant the receiver scheduler tunes to the active frequency channel.

AITE = (1/N) · ∑ (t_intercept − t_emission_start)

5. Receiver Sensitivity (Recall)

Statistical ability of the receiver policy to detect true signal presence relative to false negative misses.

Sensitivity = True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives)

6. Percentage of Correct Decisions (CPC)

Proportion of all receiver decisions that were correct (intercepting active signals or avoiding quiet channels without false alarm).

CPC = (Hits + True Negatives) / (Total Actions + Misses) × 100%

  • Periodic Pulse Radar — Fixed center frequency with deterministic pulse repetition intervals (PRI) and duty cycles.

  • Intermittent Threat — Low probability of intercept (LPI) radar with probabilistic random burst timing.
  • Burst Transmitter — High-density pulse train transmissions interspersed with long quiet periods.

🛰️ The Operational Challenge

In electronic warfare (EW) surveillance, an Electronic Support (ES) receiver must maintain continuous situational awareness across a wide radio frequency (RF) spectrum (e.g. 2–18 GHz). Because receiver hardware has an Instantaneous Bandwidth (IBW) significantly smaller than the surveillance bandwidth, the receiver must dynamically schedule discrete dwell intervals across sub-bands.

Traditional open-loop strategies rely on rigid periodic sweeping, which suffers severe pulse dropouts against modern frequency-agile radars, pulse-compression radars, and intermittent Low Probability of Intercept (LPI) emitters.

🎯 Optimal Periodic Scan Interception

For periodic emitters with pulse repetition interval Tp and phase offset φp, the optimal scan policy is deterministic pulse coincidence scheduling:

t̂k = φ̂p + k · T̂p  ⟹  Tune channel b when  t ≡ φ̂p (mod T̂p)
Coincidence Criterion

The periodic coincidence agent estimates T̂p and φ̂p via inter-pulse arrival histograms. This achieves near-100% detection probability with minimum intercept latency error while freeing intermediate dwell intervals for agile spectrum surveillance.

🧠 Reinforcement Learning Formulation (Q-Learning)

The scheduler models dwell allocation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) over contested RF channels:

  • State Space (S): Currently tuned sub-band s ∈ {0, 1, …, N−1}.
  • Action Space (A): Next sub-band to tune during dwell interval a ∈ {0, 1, …, N−1}.
  • Reward Function (R): Immediate feedback received upon dwell (R = +10 on active intercept, −2 on false alarm, −0.1 dwell cost).
Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α · [ R + γ · maxa' Q(s', a') − Q(s, a) ]
Bellman Optimality

📡 Spatially Scanning Radar Dynamics

Spatially scanning search radars sweep a narrow directional beam across 360° with rotational period Trot. The receiver intercepts the mainlobe only when beam azimuth θ(t) = (ωt + θ0) mod 360° aligns with receiver angle θrx:

|θ(t) − θrx| ≤ θbeam / 2
Mainlobe Illumination

This creates intermittent periodic bursts with wide quiet intervals between antenna rotations, testing the scheduler's ability to maintain high Probability of Intercept (Pd).

⚖️ Multi-Armed Bandit Scheduler (UCB-1)

Balances exploitation of high-density emitter channels against exploration of unknown frequencies using Upper Confidence Bounds:

at = argmaxi [ μ̂i + c · √( 2 · ln(t) / Ni(t) ) ]
Auer et al. UCB-1

Guarantees logarithmic cumulative regret O(K ln T) without requiring full state-transition matrix estimation.

📊 The 7 EW Figures of Merit

Evaluates receiver scheduling efficiency across 7 standard operational dimensions:

  • Pd (Probability of Detection): Hits / Total Emission Opportunities
  • Pfa (Probability of False Alarm): False Triggers / Quiet Channel Dwells
  • AIR (Average Intercept Rate): Hits / Total Surveillance Steps
  • AITE (Average Intercept Time Error): Mean step latency from pulse onset to detection
  • Sensitivity: True Positive Rate (Recall / TPR)
  • CPC: Percentage of Correct Tuning Decisions (Accuracy)
  • R(t): Cumulative Objective Utility Score
