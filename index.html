Probability of Detection (Pd)

The statistical ratio of active emitter pulses successfully intercepted by the receiver dwell window divided by total pulse emissions across all channels.

Pd = Intercepted Pulses / Total Emitter Transmissions

Mean Intercept Latency (AITE)

The average temporal delay between the start of an emitter burst and the instant the receiver scheduler tunes to the active frequency channel.

AITE = (1/N) · ∑ (t_intercept − t_emission_start)

Average Intercept Rate (AIR)

Normalized throughput metric reflecting total intercepted signals relative to continuous mission dwell clock intervals.

AIR = Intercepted Dwells / Total Mission Steps

Optimization Reward Objective

Linear scalar reward function guiding reinforcement learning gradient updates to prioritize timely intercepts and penalize missed activity.

R(t) = +1.0 · hit − 0.1 · miss + 0.0 · quiet

Radar & Threat Taxonomy

  • Periodic Emitter — Fixed center frequency with deterministic pulse repetition intervals (PRI) and duty cycles.
  • Frequency-Agile Hopper — Pseudo-randomly switches carrier channels across dwells to evade electronic interception.
  • Intermittent Threat — Low probability of intercept (LPI) radar with probabilistic random burst timing.
  • Burst Transmitter — High-density pulse train transmissions interspersed with long quiet periods.

Electronic Warfare Receiver Scheduling & Machine Learning Foundations
Mathematical formulation of two-dimensional spectral search and reinforcement learning scheduling.

The Operational Challenge

In electronic warfare (EW) surveillance, an Electronic Support (ES) receiver must maintain situational awareness over a wide radio frequency (RF) spectrum. Because receiver hardware typically has an instantaneous bandwidth significantly smaller than the total surveillance bandwidth, the receiver must dynamically schedule dwell times across discrete sub-bands.

Traditional open-loop strategies rely on rigid periodic sweeping, which fails to adapt against modern frequency-agile radars, pulse-compression radars, and intermittent LPI emitters.

Two-Dimensional Search Problem

Signal interception is fundamentally a spatio-temporal and frequency coincidence problem: the receiver must be tuned to frequency band $f_i$ during time slot $t_j$ when the target emitter is actively radiating energy.

P(Intercept) = P(f_receiver(t) = f_emitter(t) | Emitter_active(t))

Adaptive closed-loop algorithms formulate this as a sequential Markov decision process (MDP) where the agent predicts upcoming emitter transmissions based on historical temporal correlations.

Reinforcement Learning Formulation

The scheduler models band selection as an agent interacting with the RF environment:

  • State Space (S): Currently tuned frequency sub-band $s \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$.
  • Action Space (A): Next sub-band to tune during dwell interval $a \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$.
  • Reward Function (R): Immediate feedback received based on signal presence upon tuning.

Open-Loop vs. Closed-Loop Performance

Open-Loop (Sequential, Priority): Fixed pre-mission schedules. High coverage against steady uncoordinated continuous emitters, but poor efficiency against agile hopping and low-duty radar pulses.

Closed-Loop (Q-Learning, UCB Bandit): Online learning from live signal hits/misses. Successfully identifies radar pulse repetition intervals and synchronizes receiver dwell switching to match emitter duty cycles.
