Standard defense electronic warfare evaluation criteria for wideband intercept receivers.

1. Probability of Detection (Pd)

Ratio of active emitter pulses successfully intercepted by the receiver dwell window divided by total pulse emissions across all channels.

Pd = Intercepted Pulses / Total Active Transmissions

2. Probability of False Alarm (Pfa)

Rate of false alarm triggers occurring when receiver dwells on quiet channels due to receiver noise floor spikes.

Pfa = False Detections / Total Quiet Dwells

3. Average Intercept Rate (AIR)

Normalized interception throughput metric reflecting total intercepted signals relative to continuous mission dwell clock intervals.

AIR = Intercepted Dwells / Total Mission Steps

4. Average Intercept Time Error (AITE)

Average temporal delay between the start of an emitter burst and the instant the receiver scheduler tunes to the active frequency channel.

AITE = (1/N) · ∑ (t_intercept − t_emission_start)

5. Receiver Sensitivity (Recall)

Statistical ability of the receiver policy to detect true signal presence relative to false negative misses.

Sensitivity = True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives)

6. Percentage of Correct Decisions (CPC)

Proportion of all receiver decisions that were correct (intercepting active signals or avoiding quiet channels without false alarm).

CPC = (Hits + True Negatives) / (Total Actions + Misses) × 100%

Radar & Threat Taxonomy

  • Periodic Pulse Radar — Fixed center frequency with deterministic pulse repetition intervals (PRI) and duty cycles.
  • Spatial Scanning Radar — Rotates directional antenna beam 360°, illuminating receiver only during azimuth alignment.
  • Frequency-Agile Hopper — Pseudo-randomly switches carrier channels across dwells to evade electronic interception.
  • Intermittent Threat — Low probability of intercept (LPI) radar with probabilistic random burst timing.
  • Burst Transmitter — High-density pulse train transmissions interspersed with long quiet periods.

The Operational Challenge

In electronic warfare (EW) surveillance, an Electronic Support (ES) receiver must maintain situational awareness over a wide radio frequency (RF) spectrum. Because receiver hardware typically has an instantaneous bandwidth significantly smaller than the total surveillance bandwidth, the receiver must dynamically schedule dwell times across discrete sub-bands.

Traditional open-loop strategies rely on rigid periodic sweeping, which fails against modern frequency-agile radars, pulse-compression radars, and intermittent LPI emitters.

Optimal Periodic Scan Interception

For periodic emitters with pulse repetition interval $T_p$ and phase offset $\phi_p$, the optimal scan strategy is pulse coincidence scheduling:

\hat{t}_{k} = \hat{\phi}_p + k \cdot \hat{T}_p \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Tune Receiver to } b \text{ when } t \equiv \hat{\phi}_p \pmod{\hat{T}_p}

The periodic coincidence agent estimates $\hat{T}_p$ and $\hat{\phi}_p$ via inter-pulse arrival histograms, achieving minimum intercept latency error while freeing intermediate slots for agile spectrum search.

Reinforcement Learning Formulation

The scheduler models band selection as an agent interacting with the RF environment:

  • State Space (S): Currently tuned frequency sub-band $s \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$.
  • Action Space (A): Next sub-band to tune during dwell interval $a \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$.
  • Reward Function (R): Immediate feedback received based on signal presence upon tuning.

Spatially Scanning Radar Dynamics

Spatially scanning search radars sweep a narrow directional beam across $360^\circ$ with rotational period $T_{\text{rot}}$. The receiver intercepts the mainlobe only when the beam azimuth $\theta(t) = (\omega t + \theta_0) \pmod{360^\circ}$ aligns with the receiver angle.

|\theta(t) - \theta_{\text{rx}}| \le \frac{\theta_{\text{beam}}}{2}
